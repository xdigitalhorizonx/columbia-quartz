I.

The kitchens

Seven kitchens, from a 1920s farmhouse to a post-and-beam modern. Same idea every time: pick the slab first, then build the room around it.

No. 01 Kitchens

The Updated Farmhouse

Warm white shaker doors, unlacquered brass and a wide oak floor: the farmhouse kitchen, grown up. Classic Gold's soft gold veins pick up the brass and keep an all-white kitchen from feeling cold.

Styling notes

  1. Run the same slab up the wall behind the range. It reads calmer than tile and wipes clean in one pass.
  2. Choose a warm white paint, not a blue-white, so the gold veining looks intentional.
  3. Let unlacquered brass age. Its patina and the veining warm up together.
Surface
Calacatta Classic Gold No. 06
Edge
Eased square, 3 cm
Pairs with
Warm white paint, unlacquered brass, white oak

No. 02 Kitchens

After Dark

Matte black cabinetry needs a surface with nerve. Steel's broad gray veins hold their own against it, and a mitred waterfall end lets them pour straight down to the floor.

Styling notes

  1. Ask for the veins to continue across the mitre, so the island reads as one solid block.
  2. In a dark kitchen the white stone becomes the light. Keep the counters clear and let it work.
  3. Smoked oak and black leather warm up the contrast without adding color.
Surface
Calacatta Steel No. 05
Edge
Mitred waterfall
Pairs with
Matte black, smoked oak, black hardware

No. 03 Kitchens

Quiet Minimal

When the cabinetry is handleless oak, the counter should almost disappear. LX Classic's misty, barely-there veining gives you the depth of stone without any noise.

Styling notes

  1. Go thin. A 2 cm slab with a square edge keeps the profile crisp.
  2. Carry the counter up as the backsplash to get rid of grout lines.
  3. Keep three things on the counter, not ten. Minimal is mostly restraint.
Surface
Calacatta LX Classic No. 02
Edge
Square, 2 cm
Pairs with
Rift white oak, lime plaster, brushed steel

No. 04 Kitchens

The Estate Kitchen

A formal kitchen built around the range. LX Supreme's cloudy gray movement reads like Statuario marble, so one tall slab behind the range becomes the centerpiece.

Styling notes

  1. Place the slab's strongest movement behind the range. See your slab before it's cut.
  2. Soft gray-green paint and polished nickel let the stone stay the star.
  3. Glass-front uppers on each side keep a big alcove from feeling heavy.
Surface
Calacatta LX Supreme No. 03
Edge
Eased, 3 cm
Pairs with
French gray-green, polished nickel, herringbone oak

No. 05 Kitchens

City Galley

In a narrow pre-war galley every surface counts. Classic Silver's fine linear veining, run lengthwise, pulls the eye down the room and makes it feel longer.

Styling notes

  1. Run linear veins along a galley, never across it. Across, they chop the room up.
  2. Sage green and brass soften a bright counter in an older building.
  3. Open shelves on one side only keep a narrow room from closing in.
Surface
Calacatta Classic Silver No. 09
Edge
Eased, 3 cm
Pairs with
Sage green, brass, walnut, white hex tile

No. 06 Kitchens

Coastal Light

A light kitchen doesn't need a white counter. Classic Pearl's warm greige sits easily beside limed oak and limewash, and hides everyday crumbs far better than bright white.

Styling notes

  1. Warm neutrals forgive. Pearl hides water spots and crumbs better than stark white.
  2. A short backsplash ledge holds the oil and salt and lets the plaster wall breathe.
  3. Lean on texture: limewash, linen, handmade ceramics. It keeps a pale room from going flat.
Surface
Calacatta Classic Pearl No. 10
Edge
Eased, 3 cm
Pairs with
Limed oak, limewash, unlacquered brass

No. 07 Kitchens

Mid-Century Walnut

Walnut, terrazzo and a beamed ceiling ask for something graphic. LX Noir's bold charcoal veins bring the drama of a vintage marble without the upkeep.

Styling notes

  1. Bold stone needs simple doors. Slab-front walnut lets the veining lead.
  2. Repeat the black once or twice, in stools or a light, so the veins feel connected.
  3. Go big on the island. A long run shows off the full sweep of the vein.
Surface
Calacatta LX Noir No. 04
Edge
Mitred waterfall, 3 cm
Pairs with
Walnut, terrazzo, black accents
II.

The baths

Quartz earns its place in a bathroom. It never needs sealing, shrugs off soap and steam, and large slabs mean far fewer grout lines to scrub.

No. 08 Baths

The Primary Bath

A primary bath should feel like an exhale. Classic Arctic's bright white and fine hairline veins keep a long double vanity crisp, and a tall quartz backsplash means no grout behind the taps.

Styling notes

  1. Take the quartz up the wall behind the faucets: one surface, nothing to scrub.
  2. Fluted oak adds texture so an all-white bath doesn't feel clinical.
  3. Wall-mounted faucets leave the counter clear and quick to wipe.
Surface
Calacatta Classic Arctic No. 07
Edge
Eased, 3 cm
Pairs with
Fluted white oak, brushed nickel, limewash

No. 09 Baths

The Jewel-Box Powder Room

The smallest room in the house is the place to be bold. A floating vanity built from mitred LX Gold, against deep green plaster, turns a powder room into a jewel box.

Styling notes

  1. Mitred corners let the gold veins wrap the box, so it looks carved from one block.
  2. Dark walls make white stone glow. Green, ink or oxblood all suit gold veining.
  3. A small room needs little stone, so a statement slab is an easy splurge.
Surface
Calacatta LX Gold No. 01
Edge
Mitred box
Pairs with
Forest green plaster, unlacquered brass

No. 10 Baths

The Slab Shower

Full quartz slabs on the shower walls mean almost no grout lines, and a bench and niche cut from the same stone make the whole shower look built, not tiled.

Styling notes

  1. Quartz is non-porous, so slab walls resist mildew and never need sealing.
  2. Plan the niche during slab layout so the veining runs through it.
  3. A curbless entry and one glass panel let the stone read wall to wall.
Surface
Calacatta Classic Supreme No. 08

Eased

III.

Beyond the kitchen

Laundry rooms, pantries, fireplaces. Anywhere a surface takes a beating and still has to look good.

No. 11 Beyond the kitchen

Laundry & Mudroom

The hardest-working room deserves a surface that shrugs off detergent, wet boots and folding marathons. A counter over the machines turns a laundry closet into a real workroom.

Styling notes

  1. Run one counter over front-loaders for a full-length folding surface.
  2. Pick a patterned white. Fine veining hides lint and water marks.
  3. Blue-gray cabinets and black hardware forgive scuffs from everyday traffic.
Surface
Calacatta Classic Silver No. 09
Edge
Eased, 3 cm
Pairs with
Blue-gray paint, matte black, terracotta tile

No. 12 Beyond the kitchen

The Butler’s Pantry

Between the kitchen and the dining room, a butler's pantry makes coffee in the morning and drinks at night. LX Gold with inky blue cabinets and bronze glass doors makes it feel like a small bar.

Styling notes

  1. Quartz resists coffee rings, red wine and lemon, which is why it beats marble at a bar.
  2. Glass-front uppers show off glassware and keep a narrow room open.
  3. Take the slab up the back wall. Splashes wipe straight off.
Surface
Calacatta LX Gold No. 01
Edge
Eased, 3 cm
Pairs with
Inky blue, aged brass, dark bronze

No. 13 Beyond the kitchen

The Fireplace Wall

Quartz isn't only for kitchens and baths. A floor-to-ceiling LX Supreme surround with a floating hearth bench gives a modern fireplace the presence of carved marble.

Styling notes

  1. Use quartz for the surround, not the firebox. Follow your fireplace's heat clearances.
  2. Book-match the slabs so the veining flows across the seams.
  3. A cantilevered hearth doubles as extra seating by the fire.
Surface
Calacatta LX Supreme No. 03
Edge
Mitred hearth bench
Pairs with
White oak built-ins, boucle, wool
